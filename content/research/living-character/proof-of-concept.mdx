## Is this the pure creature?

Mostly, but not purely. The learning is real, and the steering around it is partly hand-written. The clearest evidence is what happens when that steering is removed:

| Same body, same seed, 1,800 minutes | Needs in range | Time walking | Reached desk and fridge |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core with the hand-written steering (shown above) | 96.4% | 49% | Yes |
| Core choosing every action alone | 0% | 71% | No |

Alone, his work and social needs fell to zero and stayed there. This is one seed on the current core, so it's a clear signal and not a measured rate. On this body, the competence you see depends on the steering.

## How he learned where to go

Nobody told him what a fridge does. The core never sees the words fridge, couch or energy. It gets 8 unlabelled numbers, 3 needs with an acceptable range, and 5 actions that are just numbered options.

1. **He lives.** Early on, he acts on an untrained habit, which takes him to different places.
2. **He predicts.** After every minute, a forward model compares what it expected with what happened, and learns from the difference. This is how "action 1, then standing there for a while, makes need 0 rise" gets learned.
3. **He imagines.** Every 40 minutes, for each of the 5 options, he simulates heading there and staying for 36 minutes, across 16 imagined versions of the future. He picks the option with the lowest expected need cost.
4. **He rehearses.** After 500 minutes, imagined comparisons from real remembered moments also train his habit. Real outcomes don't train it directly, because that taught him superstitions in earlier tests.

So the link between the fridge and energy is learned from his own life. Going to the fridge when tired is never written anywhere.

## What's learned and what's written

| Part | Who decides it |
|---|---|
| What each place does to his needs, as he understands it | **Learned** from experience |
| Which place he picks each time | **Learned** model, scored by imagination |
| His habit | **Learned** through rehearsal |
| How the world works: walking speed, decay, what each place gives | Written. It's his world |
| Having 3 needs and their acceptable range | Written. It's what he is |
| Considering a new choice every 40 minutes | **Written steering** |
| Stopping once a need is "comfortably back", with one threshold per place | **Written steering** |
| Giving up on a plan after 60 minutes | **Written steering** |
| Going to the door when a visitor arrives | **Written steering** |
| Only comparing "go there and stay" as plans | **Written steering** |

In this recording, he was following one of these plans for 65% of the time, and his habit moved him the rest of the time. His social need still dropped as low as 0.22, below the 0.35 limit.

## With language and memory on

The full version adds a language model that speaks for him, plus memory and name recognition. These results are from an earlier version of the core, whose saved life no longer loads:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Needs in range, random walk | 0.000 |
| Needs in range, habit alone | 0.000 |
| Needs in range, habit and imagined plans | 0.974 (paired t 47.8, 6 seeds × 2,500 minutes) |
| Returning visitor's name | Recognised across a restart |
| False memory: "pizza you brought yesterday" | Held back by the grounding gate |

The first gate let a false memory through by citing the visitor's own question. That was fixed and pinned with a test. Those imagined plans use the same written steering described above.

## Limits

Thinking takes 25–45 seconds on a laptop. Familiarity recognises exact words but not near-spellings. The next real step is making the core's own choices work in this body, so the steering can be removed.
