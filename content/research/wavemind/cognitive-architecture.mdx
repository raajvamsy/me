## The question

Measured fairly against real small models, what is this good for?

## Next-token prediction

Lower is better.

| Predictor | Parameters | Perplexity |
|---|---|---:|
| Tiny transformer | About 250k | 52.3 |
| Tiny GRU | About 100k | 93.1 |
| KN-bigram | Counts | 145.4 |
| WaveMind, best version | None | 161,345 |

WaveMind loses by a huge margin, and its added modules barely changed this.

## Learning a second language

English perplexity before and after learning Japanese:

| Model | Before | After | Forgetting |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bigram | 199 | 201 | 1.01× |
| KN-bigram | 61 | 81 | 1.33× |
| WaveMind | 236,262 | 931,952 | 3.94× |
| Tiny GRU | 120 | 1,000,000 | 8,353× |
| Tiny transformer | 60 | 1,000,000 | 16,763× |

The neural models collapse, while WaveMind degrades far less. Simple count models do better still, which keeps the claim modest.

## Also in the paper

Hierarchical wave clusters, capacity-limited working memory, intrinsic predictive learning, and cognitive psychology tests. On Saffran word segmentation it was at chance, 0% discrimination, against 100% for the tiny GRU, 94% for the tiny transformer and 62.5% for the bigram models.

The paper is a draft and has not been published.
