## The question

Could the unchanged core learn to make a sound it hears? It gets no teacher and no labels. It sees unlabelled numbers, has 3 bounded actions (voice, jaw and tongue), and has one need: to sound like the recording.

The core wasn't changed for any of these bodies. Only the body was built, and its rules were frozen before any run.

## Attempt 1: six vowels with a listener

The first body had a listener that answered yes or no, and being heard correctly raised a "fed" need. It failed twice, and adding a guided hand didn't help.

| Why it failed | Detail |
|---|---|
| The signal was a needle | A random mouth was heard correctly about 2.5% of the time |
| The need pinned at 0 | Rare successes were erased |
| A right vowel was worth little | Only 3% of the need's range |

## Attempt 2: one vowel by ear

So I changed how the body hears, and not the core. The ear became graded: every closer sound scores higher, and the need tracks recent likeness and never pins.

| Life | Perfect at tick | Likeness, first → last 1000 | Final mouth F1 / F2 (best 1090 / 1625 Hz) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 3,500 | 0.39 → 0.90 | 1098 / 1649 |
| 2 | 5,500 | 0.44 → 0.94 | 1092 / 1621 |
| 3 | 5,000 | 0.38 → 0.93 | 1104 / 1618 |
| Random, for reference | Never | 0.19 | Not applicable |

**Perfect in 3 of 3 lives,** with the final mouth within 2–14 Hz of the best possible position in the first band and within about 24 Hz in the second. One of the pre-run checks did fail: the ear had a tiny plateau where a single basin was expected. I reported it and didn't tune it, and no life got stuck there.

## Attempt 3: a sentence, speaking along

A recording of "Hi, how are you?" plays in a loop, and the creature hears each slice along with its own voice. It has to change its mouth every 77 ms and stay quiet in the gaps.

The body borrows the speaker's throat, so it can sound like her even with a wrong mouth. That's why results are judged by the numbers and against a fixed-mouth reference, not by ear alone.

| Measure | Best stretch, across 3 lives | Fixed mouth, perfect timing | Random |
|---|---|---|---|
| Likeness | 0.88–0.89 | 0.85 | 0.40 |
| Timing right | 98–99.5% | 100% | 50% |
| Worse than best mouth | 1.3–1.8 dB | 2.7 dB | 3.6 dB |

**It didn't reach the frozen bar** of within 1 dB and in range 95% of the time, with 0 of 3 lives perfect. At its best it matched every slice within about 0.6–3.3 dB. Then, between about ticks 6,000 and 10,000, every life collapsed to near-random timing and never recovered.

## Why it collapsed

My first explanation was that it forgot what mistakes cost. An exact replay with read-only observers refuted that.

| What I checked | Finding |
|---|---|
| Memory | Intact. Nothing was erased before the end of life |
| World model | Still right throughout: it knew silence on her sound and speech in her silence both cost |
| Habit | Broken. Its exploration spread fell from 1.0 to 0.05 |

The habit stopped exploring, so its rehearsal candidates all landed on the same side of speaking or staying quiet. Rehearsal could no longer test what the world model already knew, and noise walked the habit away from the right answer.

I also made a mistake along the way. I first reported that it had learned nothing, because I compared only the first and last 1,000 ticks. The lesson was to always look at the whole curve.

## What it led to

The diagnosis led to the core's S1 change: the habit's spread now narrows only on credible evidence. The next step would be several vowels, or re-learning after the target changes mid-life, with graded hearing throughout. Neither has started.
